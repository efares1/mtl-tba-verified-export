\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
\emph{Internal validity.} The theorems concern the Rocq model of automata; the extracted OCaml functions are the verified ones, but the surrounding tool is not verified: the Spot call, the LBTT reader, the pretty printer and the UPPAAL XML writer are trusted. We reduce this risk with a second printer and a reader check (\texttt{-check}), and with independent exploration of the case-study models, but these are tests, not proofs. Measured sizes depend on Spot's output for the same formula; we fix the Spot version and options.

\emph{Construct validity.} CASAAL implements the semantics of Li et al.\ with integer bounds and mutually exclusive events, and it reports transition-based acceptance. We compare only configurations whose CASAAL encoding is exact in that setting, translate its transition acceptance to location acceptance before comparing sizes, and report TChecker's stored states, which is a property of the pair (automaton, checker), not of the automaton alone. Run times of the checker are below a millisecond on these inputs and are not used as evidence.

\emph{External validity.} The requirement patterns are a small corpus drawn from the literature and from parameterized families; the two system models are small. Results on the conjunctive families (R) show a trend with the number of conjuncts but do not predict behavior on industrial specifications. A matched TChecker comparison before and after optimization of our own output, on two response systems, shows the same exploration (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}); we make no speedup claim from optimization alone.

\emph{Conclusion validity.} Every number in the tables is produced by scripts in the archive from raw tool output; the comparisons are counts over a fixed list of configurations, and we do not apply significance tests to them.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented a Rocq-verified optimization and export pipeline for timed B\"uchi automata and its composition with a verified MTL translation. Each pass preserves the timed-word language of every input TBA, the optimizer does so for every number of rounds, and the output of export has conjunctive guards and invariants as UPPAAL requires. The passes are extracted to OCaml and run in \texttt{mtl2tba}. In comparison with the independent tool CASAAL, our automata have equal or smaller size on 25 of 27 exactly encodable configurations once acceptance is compared on equal terms, and for the negated requirements make TChecker store fewer states on most of them, with identical verdicts everywhere. We also reported the limits: explicit conjunctive export can multiply edges, and optimization alone did not change TChecker's exploration on two system models.

Future work includes a verified pass that merges simulation-equivalent locations, which would close the remaining gap to CASAAL on lower-bounded responses; symbolic handling of Boolean transition labels before Spot expansion; generalized B\"uchi acceptance on transitions; and evaluation on independent industrial TBA corpora.
