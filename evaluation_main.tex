\subsection{Questions, evidence, and setup}
This section evaluates the whole translation from an MTL requirement to the automaton given to a timed model checker, and the optimization passes inside it. It asks four questions. How does the translation compare, tool to tool, with CASAAL in the size of the automaton, in generation time and in the cost the automaton imposes on a timed model checker (Section~\ref{sec:casaal-eval})? Which stage is responsible for the difference? What does conversion to conjunctive guards and invariants cost? What does verification add that testing does not (Section~\ref{sec:verification-adds})? Internal output shape is a Rocq theorem; checker verdicts, sizes and times are experimental evidence.

We retain the 31-attempt F/R/N benchmark as an upstream-generated group; 29 configurations completed the upstream stage and supplied an optimizer input (R7 and R8 timed out, and with the later implementation of Section~\ref{sec:speedups} their positive forms complete in 14~s and 90~s). Rewriting, Spot configuration, cube expansion and reset completion happen upstream and are not credited to the optimizer. The end-to-end theorem for the default front end is \path{MTL_to_exported_correct0_recur_weak_with}; the optimizer theorems hold for every input TBA of the interface. Results are medians of five runs with a 300~s limit, on Ubuntu 26.04/WSL~2, an Intel Core i7-8650U at 1.9~GHz with 11.7~GB of memory, OCaml 5.4.0, Rocq 9.0.1 and Spot 2.16. The generated-group numbers were measured with Spot level \texttt{small} and the implementation before the speedups; the run log and generated automata are under \path{evaluation/runs/2026-10-14-full-eval-v3/}.

Across the 29 completed inputs the reachable transitions fall from 20,204 after reset completion to 1,500 after optimization (92.58\%), but this is skewed: R6 accounts for 14,985 of the 18,704 removed, and the six bounded-response R cases for 98.6\%. Export then grows the explicit transitions from 1,500 to 3,209, a representation cost reported separately. With symbolic grouping in both runs, the optimizer alone lowers the symbolic transitions from 704 to 344 on the 27 configurations whose unoptimized export completes (smaller on 17, equal on 10). The largest case is R6: 64 Spot states and 15,625 cube-split transitions become 640 edges and six clocks, and export produces 1,837 explicit edges with 63 invariant-bearing locations. The per-instance and aggregate tables are in Supplement Section~\ref{S-sec:generated} and Supplement Section~\ref{S-sec:eval-extra}.

\subsection{Matched comparison of the two export policies}
\label{sec:export-policies}
Holding the optimized automaton fixed on R2--R6, all-copy export adds no explicit edges beyond the optimized relation and no diagonal guards, while maximal-delay export adds edges in each case and between 2 and 3,360 diagonal guard atoms per formula (4,464 in total); location, clock and invariant counts are the same, and exporter times have no consistent order (Supplement Table~\ref{S-tab:generated-export-policy}). This is a property of this benchmark family, not a general size theorem.

The policy matters to a model checker by whether it can read the automaton. TChecker's liveness algorithms (\texttt{tck-liveness -a couvscc}) reject diagonal constraints (``unsupported diagonal constraints''). On the negated requirements the two policies give identical automata and TChecker counts; on the positive forms the maximal-delay export contains diagonal guards from R2 on, and TChecker stops with this error on each, while the all-copy export is accepted. We composed the positive automaton of Rn with a system that violates the requirement on every run, so that the product is empty and explored completely. With the all-copy export TChecker stores 48 states for each of R2--R6; with CASAAL's automaton it stores 433, 4{,}225, 44{,}389 and 485{,}833 on R2--R5 (Supplement Table~\ref{S-tab:baseline-posbad}) and does not finish R6 within 120~s. The constant 48 is not due to the location invariants of our export: removing them gives 52 states, and giving CASAAL's automaton the invariant script or our export gives 375, 3{,}439 and 34{,}107 on R2--R4. The difference lies in the automaton that the translation produces for the positive form, which we did not analyse further, on a workload that we constructed.


\subsection{Translator-independent synthetic suite}
\label{sec:synthetic}
To exercise the interface without the MTL translator, we run the optimizer and export on 16 small synthetic TBAs and on parameterized families written as Rocq records. They cover redundant and contradictory guards, dead resets, synchronously reset clocks, duplicate transitions, equivalent locations and a disjunctive-invariant export. The suite is synthetic and is no evidence of industrial performance; it shows that each pass acts on the structure it targets and that the passes interact: omitting forward propagation retains all seven edges of the forward cascade where the full optimizer leaves four, and normalizing a tautological $x\ge0$ guard is what lets dead-reset removal drop a clock (Supplement Table~\ref{S-tab:synthetic-interactions}). On the 100 random formulas of the translation article the optimizer reduces transitions on 50 (3,980 to 2,366 in total) and increases none (Supplement Section~\ref{S-sec:random}); the remaining tables are in Supplement Section~\ref{S-sec:synthetic-detail}.


\subsection{Model checking, agreement with the semantics, and what verification adds}
\label{sec:verification-adds}
\emph{UPPAAL.} We generated 311 UPPAAL~5.0.0 models: the negated requirement in seven variants (ours with the default, \texttt{-n 0} and all-copy exports; CASAAL's automaton converted to location acceptance, plain and through our export in the same three ways) composed with request/response, parallel-component, sporadic and free systems. All 296 runs with an expected verdict return it, for every variant, and UPPAAL reads the default export with its diagonal guards. On full explorations the stored states are identical in all variants except F5 and F8 (3 and 2 states for ours against 4 and 4). Where times differ they follow the number of explicit property edges: R6 with parallel components takes 1.3~s on ours (182 edges) and 3.5~s on CASAAL's (330 edges), and CASAAL's automaton passed through our export is as slow as its own. These are small systems and reachability queries, so they show that the exported models are usable and give the same verdicts, not a general speedup (Supplement Section~\ref{S-sec:uppaal-check}, Table~\ref{S-tab:uppaal}). For timed liveness, the matched TChecker runs with and without the optimizer store the same states and explore the same product (77 and 15 states on the two cases).

\emph{Agreement with the semantics.} An independent evaluator of the pointwise MTL semantics, transcribed from the Rocq definition, together with acceptance evaluators for our UPPAAL XML and for CASAAL's DOT output, was run on random ultimately periodic timed words (22 benchmark patterns with 20{,}000 words each and 100 random formulas with 6{,}000 words each, both polarities). Our tool with both export policies and CASAAL agree with the evaluator on all 5{,}960{,}000 word checks. The evaluator rejects 11 of 13 and 7 of 7 deliberately wrong formulas for our tool and for CASAAL. This finds no bug in either tool; it is agreement on a finite sample of a restricted word class (Supplement Section~\ref{S-sec:differential}).

\emph{Seeded defects.} Agreement does not show what the proofs add. We therefore injected 22 plausible defects, one at a time, in a scratch copy of the extracted optimizer and exporter (a strict instead of non-strict test at the bound zero, a reset set not compared when two locations merge, the complement of $x<c$ taken as $x>c$) and tested each in two ways: by comparing its output with the unmutated tool's on the benchmark and a corpus of 148 generated automata, and by testing, on 100{,}000 random words per input, whether the output accepts the words of its input. Seventeen mutants change the language of some automaton. The word test finds 13 of them. Four, M01, M10, M12 and M16, pass both tests: the benchmark never reaches the defect, and the word test, which misses even a single dropped transition in 36\% of our controls, does not hit the few words that separate the languages. For each we built an automaton on which the languages differ (for M01, 852 accepted sample words become none), so these are real defects that a test of this kind misses. The oracle is itself unverified, and a stronger test may find some of them. The other five mutants do not change the language (M17 is argued, not proved). Applying the same mistakes to the Rocq definitions in a scratch copy of the development (the unmutated copy compiles in about 43~s with the four standard axioms), the development rejects all four survivors within 3 to 5 seconds, at \texttt{inv\_guard\_w\_eq}, \texttt{sim\_spec}, \texttt{compl\_cases} and \texttt{const\_true\_spec}, and the three further mutants we tried (M13, M14, M19); we did not run the other 15. The check is as strong as the specification it compares against: a mutation of implementation and specification together would pass. The claim is therefore bounded: for these defects in this code, a 100{,}000-word test missed four that the development rejects. It does not show a defect in CASAAL, which agreed with the evaluator throughout (Supplement Section~\ref{S-sec:mutation}, Tables~\ref{S-tab:mutants} and~\ref{S-tab:rocq-rejection}).

\subsection{Limitations}
TChecker states and UPPAAL verdicts are measured on small products: R4--R7 and N5--N6 did not finish for either tool within 300~s, and for R8, N7 and N8 no product could be generated. The state-count advantage over CASAAL as encoded for TChecker is a property of the acceptance encoding (Section~\ref{sec:casaal-ablation}); the importer used for that ablation is outside the proofs, and the difference on the positive-form workload is not explained. The mutation study uses 22 hand-chosen defects and an unverified test oracle, with Rocq-level transcriptions for seven of them. Spot, on a clocked formula of size $3^k$ for depth $k$, is what makes us slower than CASAAL on deep nests; we built no alternative front end. The theorems are universal only over the Rocq TBA interface and its timed-word semantics, not through an unverified adapter to arbitrary file formats.
