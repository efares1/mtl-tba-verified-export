Prior work already contains several of the algorithmic ideas used here. Daws and Yovine combine active-clock detection with detection of clocks that remain equal and evaluate the state-space reductions~\cite{daws_yovine_rtss1996}; Herrera, Westphal, and Podelski extend equal-clock reduction to quasi-equal clocks~\cite{herrera_westphal_podelski_tacas2014}. We do not claim that removing inactive clocks or merging equal clocks is new; the Rocq development proves its dead-reset and synchronous-reset transformations as timed-word language equivalences and composes them with the other passes. Guha, Narayan, and Arun-Kumar construct a timed-bisimilar automaton with the fewest possible clocks, at doubly exponential worst-case cost~\cite{guha_narayan_arunkumar_concur2014}, and Yalcinkaya and Aydin Gol give a position-based reduction that preserves design parameters~\cite{yalcinkaya_aydin_gol_formalise2019}. These are stronger objectives than our synchronous-reset merge, which does not minimize clock count; neither article describes a mechanization.

Badban, Leue, and Smaus derive control-location invariants from existing invariants and incoming constraints and prune idle transitions~\cite{badban_etal_wing2010}, for predicate abstraction rather than under a B\"uchi-language preservation contract. They note that UPPAAL admits no disjunctive location invariants, approximate such joins by \texttt{true}, and mention location splitting as a direction without giving the construction. De Wulf, Doyen, and Raskin copy locations in a compositional encoding of the almost-ASAP semantics~\cite{de_wulf_doyen_raskin_fm2005}, with a correctness theorem for a specialized controller-safety semantics, and Bodeveix and Filali informally propose a one-copy-per-disjunct split for two disjuncts, announcing a PVS proof~\cite{[BF03]}. We make no priority claim for location splitting; we prove two split policies for our formal interface and introduce no silent labels. Our searches found no mechanized composition of timed-automata clock reduction and invariant transformations.

Mechanized timed-automata work addresses adjacent guarantees: Wimmer and Lammich refine reachability checking to an executable Isabelle/HOL model checker~\cite{munta_tacas18,munta_formats19}, and Wimmer, Herbreteau, and van de Pol verify a certifier for emptiness certificates of timed B\"uchi automata, including certificates from TChecker and Imitator~\cite{wimmer_herbreteau_vandepol_formats2020}. They verify checking or certification, not the optimization and representation transformations proved here.

Translators from real-time logics are the closest tools to the whole pipeline. CASAAL~\cite{casaal_tool} implements the construction of Li et al.~\cite{li_spin7} for MTL$_{0,\infty}$, the pointwise semantics we use, with Boolean transition labels and transition-based acceptance; we compare with it in size, generation time and stored states (Sections~\ref{sec:casaal-size}--\ref{sec:casaal-tck}). MightyL~\cite{mightyl_paper17} translates MITL through very-weak one-clock alternating automata; to our knowledge it does not target the pointwise MTL$_{0,\infty}$ semantics, so we did not run it. As far as we could find, none of these translators has a machine-checked correctness proof. Our chain adds no new construction, and in particular no new clock-reduction or minimization algorithm; what distinguishes it is its structure and its proofs. The large difference in stored states that we measure on the positive form of the bounded responses (Section~\ref{sec:casaal-stress}) concerns the structure of the automata that the two translators generate, not a verified optimization. Each stage is stated against an interface, so the propositional backend or the whole front end can be replaced without touching the optimization and export theorems (Section~\ref{sec:modular}). The defensible claim is mechanization and composition, supported by the seeded-defect study of Section~\ref{sec:verification-adds}; the benchmark and synthetic results support benchmark-specific claims only, not broad format coverage or universal efficiency.
