Real-time requirements in Metric Temporal Logic are checked by translating their negation into a timed B\"uchi automaton (TBA) that a model checker such as UPPAAL or TChecker composes with the system. We present a modular chain from MTL requirements to timed automata whose optimization and export stages are verified in Rocq, and evaluate it, tool to tool, against the translator CASAAL. The passes preserve the timed-word language of every TBA over the formula-indexed clock interface, and an end-to-end theorem composes them with a verified MTL translation; the tool runs Spot under a backend contract, and Spot, its readers and the XML printer remain trusted. For the negated requirements, our automata have the same size as CASAAL's except on three patterns, where they are smaller; for the requirements as written they are smaller on four of 27 patterns and larger on two. The model-checking cost is almost the same: over 18 products TChecker stores 204,564 states on ours and 204,819 on CASAAL's converted to location acceptance, and UPPAAL stores identical state counts on all but two products. Generation is slower than CASAAL's on deeply nested requirements. What the chain adds is assurance that tests can miss: of 22 seeded defects in the extracted code, 18 change the language; a random-word test finds 13, a fuzz 25 times larger finds four of the other five, and Rocq rejects all 22 within seconds. The baselines are weak tests, and the specification is not checked independently; an independent MTL evaluator finds no disagreement with either tool on 6,160,000 word checks.
