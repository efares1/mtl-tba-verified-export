\section{End-to-End Guarantee for MTL Requirements}
\label{sec:composition}
The passes of Sections~\ref{sec:optimization}--\ref{sec:export} are stated over arbitrary TBAs. For MTL requirements they are used behind a translation $\mathsf{compile\_with}(A)$ that turns a propositional B\"uchi automaton $A$ for the clocked-LTL encoding $\T(f)$ of an MTL$_{0,\infty}$ formula $f$ into a TBA. Its correctness, in the form we need, is the result of~\cite{faresmtl}: if $A$ accepts exactly the action-consistent propositional words that satisfy $\T(f)$ (the \emph{backend contract}) and $f$ is well formed (nonnegative non-strict upper bounds, strictly positive bounds for the other timed operators), then
\[
\mathsf{msat}(w,0,f)\ \leftrightarrow\ \mathsf{TBA\_accepts}(\mathsf{compile\_with}(A),w)
\]
for every timed word $w$ with nondecreasing timestamps. The same work provides a propositional automaton $\mathsf{backend}(f,\T(f))$ for which the backend contract is proved in Rocq.

Our development imports these definitions and results, and instantiates the generic composition with that backend:
\begin{theorem}[End-to-end correctness with the verified backend]
\label{thm:composition}
For every $n\in\Nat$, every well-formed MTL$_{0,\infty}$ formula $f$ and every timed word $w$,
\[
  \mathsf{msat}(w,0,f)\ \leftrightarrow\
  \mathsf{DTA\_accepts}\bigl(\export(\optimize(n,\mathsf{compile\_with}(\mathsf{backend}(f,\T(f))))),\,w\bigr).
\]
The same holds with the all-copy exporter of Section~\ref{sec:export} in place of $\export$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $C=\mathsf{compile\_with}(\mathsf{backend}(f,\T(f)))$, $O_n=\optimize(n,C)$ and $D_n=\export(O_n)$. The translation result gives $\mathsf{msat}(w,0,f)\leftrightarrow\mathsf{TBA\_accepts}(C,w)$, the backend contract being discharged by the verified backend. Preservation by optimization (Theorem~\ref{thm:optimization}) gives $\mathsf{TBA\_accepts}(C,w)\leftrightarrow\mathsf{TBA\_accepts}(O_n,w)$, and preservation by export (Theorem~\ref{thm:export}) gives $\mathsf{TBA\_accepts}(O_n,w)\leftrightarrow\mathsf{DTA\_accepts}(D_n,w)$. The Rocq statements are \path{MTL_to_exported_correct_verified} and \path{MTL_to_exported_all_correct_verified}; \texttt{Print Assumptions} lists only the standard axioms of the Rocq library (classical logic, functional extensionality, constructive definite description, and the real-number axiom of the standard library).
\end{proof}
The general form \path{MTL_to_exported_correct_with} replaces the verified backend by any automaton $A$ that satisfies the backend contract, and is the form the tool relies on: it calls Spot, whose output is not covered by the theorem but is tested against the contract. The verified backend does no simplification and is a proof witness only; it is not the one used by the extracted tool.

No convergence premise is imposed on $n$: preservation is proved by induction for every finite number of rounds, so the driver's stop after a structural fixed point or after 50 rounds is a policy that does not enter the theorem. The conclusion concerns the internal exported record and the existential initial-clock semantics of Section~\ref{sec:model}. A second theorem, \path{MTL_to_exported_correct0_with}, gives the zero-initialized reading whenever a check named \texttt{init\_free} succeeds on the exported automaton; nothing guarantees that the check succeeds when acceptance relies on nonzero initial clock values. Neither theorem verifies the LBTT importer, the XML printer or parser, or UPPAAL itself (Section~\ref{sec:tool}).
