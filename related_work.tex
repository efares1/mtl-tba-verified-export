Prior work already contains several of the algorithmic ideas used here. Daws and Yovine combine active-clock detection with detection of equal clocks~\cite{daws_yovine_rtss1996}, and Herrera, Westphal, and Podelski extend equal-clock reduction to quasi-equal clocks~\cite{herrera_westphal_podelski_tacas2014}. Removing inactive clocks and merging equal clocks are not new; we prove our versions as timed-word language equivalences. Guha, Narayan, and Arun-Kumar construct a timed-bisimilar automaton with the fewest possible clocks~\cite{guha_narayan_arunkumar_concur2014}, and Yalcinkaya and Aydin Gol give a position-based reduction~\cite{yalcinkaya_aydin_gol_formalise2019}; neither describes a mechanization. Saeedloei and Kluźniak study clock allocation in timed automata by liveness, interference graphs and colouring~\cite{saeedloei_kluzniak_hscc2018}, with optimal allocation in polynomial time for a subclass; as we read it, their treatment is on paper and for restricted classes of automata. Our clock reuse (Section~\ref{sec:reuse}) uses the same ingredients and validates its certificate as for register allocation in CompCert~\cite{rideau_leroy_cc2010}; we claim no new allocation method, only a Rocq-checked guarantee for B\"uchi automata with existential initial values and a heuristic colouring (DSATUR) that need not be optimal.

Badban, Leue, and Smaus derive control-location invariants and prune idle transitions~\cite{badban_etal_wing2010}, for predicate abstraction rather than under a B\"uchi-language contract; they note that UPPAAL admits no disjunctive location invariants and mention location splitting as a direction without giving the construction. De Wulf, Doyen, and Raskin copy locations in an encoding of the almost-ASAP semantics~\cite{de_wulf_doyen_raskin_fm2005}, and Bodeveix and Filali informally propose a one-copy-per-disjunct split for two disjuncts, announcing a PVS proof~\cite{[BF03]}. We make no priority claim for location splitting; we prove two split policies for our formal interface.

Mechanized optimization of automata exists for untimed B\"uchi automata: Schimpf and Smaus formalize bisimulation reduction and $\alpha$-ball reduction in Isabelle/HOL with a verified implementation~\cite{schimpf_smaus_ila2015}, and simulation-based reductions of nondeterministic B\"uchi automata remove transitions and merge states when one simulates another~\cite{clemente_mayr_lmcs19}. These automata have no clocks, guards or invariants. Our pruning (Section~\ref{sec:prune}) differs from both: it is a relation on locations, with two clock sets per location (equal values on $\mathit{live}$, smaller or equal on $\mathit{lle}$ for upper-bound guards), so the dominating and the dominated transition may reset different clocks, and it is checked by local conditions, not computed as a timed simulation on configurations; we know of no mechanized timed counterpart in the work cited here.

Mechanized timed-automata work verifies checking or certification, not transformations: Wimmer and Lammich refine reachability checking to an executable Isabelle/HOL model checker~\cite{munta_tacas18,munta_formats19}, and Wimmer, Herbreteau, and van de Pol verify a certifier for emptiness certificates of timed B\"uchi automata~\cite{wimmer_herbreteau_vandepol_formats2020}.

Translators from real-time logics are the closest tools to the whole pipeline. CASAAL~\cite{casaal_tool} implements the construction of Li et al.~\cite{li_spin7} for MTL$_{0,\infty}$, the pointwise semantics we use, with Boolean transition labels and transition-based acceptance; we compare with it in size, generation time and stored states (Sections~\ref{sec:casaal-size}--\ref{sec:casaal-tck}). MightyL~\cite{mightyl_paper17} translates MITL through very-weak one-clock alternating automata; to our knowledge it does not target the pointwise MTL$_{0,\infty}$ semantics, so we did not run it. As far as we could find, none of these translators has a machine-checked correctness proof. Our chain adds no new translation construction; its contribution is mechanization and composition (Sections~\ref{sec:modular} and~\ref{sec:checked}), supported by the seeded-defect study of Section~\ref{sec:verification-adds}.
