Dear Editors of Array,

We submit the manuscript "Verified Optimization and Export of Timed Büchi Automata with Certificate-Checked Reductions" for consideration as a regular paper. The manuscript was submitted to the Journal of Systems and Software (manuscript JSSOFTWARE-S-26-04310) and is transferred to Array through Elsevier's Article Transfer Service on the recommendation of that journal's editor; it is not under consideration anywhere else. It fits the Software Engineering and Theoretical Computer Science areas of Array's scope.

The paper presents a framework for the verified post-processing of the timed Büchi automata (TBAs) that UPPAAL and TChecker use to check Metric Temporal Logic requirements, together with its use in an MTL-to-TBA translator. The optimization and export passes are verified in the Rocq proof assistant: they preserve the timed-word language of every TBA of the input, and the export has the conjunctive guards and invariants that UPPAAL needs. Two further reductions, the removal of dominated transitions and the reuse of clocks, run an unverified analysis whose certificate a verified checker validates, so the language is preserved whatever the analysis returns. A verified slicing of a conjunction of requirements has a weaker guarantee that needs a condition on the system. An end-to-end theorem composes the passes with a verified MTL translation. The tool runs Spot, which is trusted through an explicit contract, and the zero-initialized guarantee needs a check on the exported automaton, so the guarantee for the shipped tool is conditional while the preservation theorems of the passes are not; the manuscript states these boundaries and the trusted base.

The evaluation compares the whole translation with the independent translator CASAAL (automaton size, generation time, states stored by TChecker), checks the extracted code and the exported automata against an independent evaluator of the MTL semantics, and reports a pacemaker case study, a gateway case study, and TChecker's own train_gate example. We report the negative results as well: on the negated requirements our automata are mostly the size of CASAAL's with no model-checking gain, the optimizer and the pruning of our own automata do not change what TChecker explores, and generation is slower than CASAAL's on nested requirements. The larger effects are on requirements under assumptions: checked pruning and clock reuse take TChecker's own train_gate example with three trains from 6,036,642 to 16,299 stored states (search from 368 s to 0.3 s), and our gateway from 132,438 to 21; clock reuse and slicing are shown on constructed workloads, and the manuscript says so.

Relation to previous work. The verified translation from MTL to timed Büchi automata that we build on, including the clocked-LTL encoding and the reset completion, is described in a separate article (submitted to ACM Transactions on Computational Logic; Rocq development on Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23916521); this manuscript does not repeat its proofs. The optimization, the certificate-checked reductions, the export and their composition with that translation, the tool and the evaluation are new here. That article leaves the optimization of the automata, their export to UPPAAL and their comparison with other translators to future work; these are the subject of this manuscript.
