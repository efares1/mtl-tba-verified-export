# Experiment 4: differential test against CASAAL with an MTL judge

Directory: `evaluation/differential/`. Nothing outside this directory was modified.

## Result

**No disagreement was found.** On 22 benchmark patterns (20,000 random lasso words each) and 100
random formulas (6,000 words each), in both polarities (`f` and `!f`), the automata generated by
`mtl2tba` (default export and `-all-copy` export) and the automata produced by CASAAL accepted exactly
the words that the independent MTL evaluator says they should. There are 5,960,000 word checks in total
(each formula/word pair is checked in the positive and the negated polarity, against each tool), and every one agrees
with the judge. So there is **no evidence of a bug in CASAAL, in our tool, or of a
semantic mismatch** on this word class. This is agreement on tests, not a proof of correctness of either tool.

## Method

* **Word class.** Ultimately periodic words u v^omega: prefix 0-5 events, loop 1-4 events, one letter per
  position (a proposition of the formula or `other`), strictly increasing timestamps (all delays at least 1/2),
  time diverges (the loop has positive duration), clocks start at 0 at time 0. All times are multiples of
  1/2 (exact integer arithmetic in half-units); the first timestamp is at least 1/2 (the TChecker adapter
  convention; a first event at time 0 was not tested). Delays are drawn from 1..14 half-units plus,
  for every constant c of the formula, the values 2c, 2c+1, 2c-1, c and 2c+2 half-units, so interval bounds
  are hit exactly and one half-unit on either side. Letters are drawn with per-word skewed weights, and 40%
  of words have a constant loop, so that "q holds throughout" situations occur (`run_diff_words.py`).
* **Judge** (`dlib.py`, function `judge`). Pointwise MTL semantics read from the Rocq definition `msat`
  (`proof/MTL_to_TBA_Shared_Clock_Derived_Strict_Direct_Core.v`, from line 168): `p U_I q` at i holds iff there is
  j >= i with t_j - t_i in I, q at j, and p at every k in [i,j); the hatted operators use j > i and k in
  (i,j); release is the dual (`p R_I q = not(not p U_I not q)`, the duality proved in the development);
  `[]` and `<>` are release-with-false and until-with-true. Evaluation on the lasso uses that the suffix at
  position i >= s is the suffix at i+q up to time translation, so truth values are periodic. A scan for an
  until witness is cut off after the lower bound is met plus one full period (any later witness has an
  earlier one one period before). The parser follows the precedence of `tool/src/parser.mly`.
* **mtl2tba acceptance.** The UPPAAL XML (`Property` template, accepting locations `A*`, invariants,
  guards including diagonal constraints, resets) is run on the word: event i is read after the delay,
  the invariant of the source location must hold at the end of the stay, the guard must hold, the clocks are
  reset. Acceptance is a cycle in the loop part through an accepting location (Tarjan SCC over
  (position, location, clock state)). The clock state is the vector of values clipped at maxconst+1 together with
  pairwise differences clipped at +-(maxconst+1), which is exact for guards with constants up to maxconst,
  also for diagonal guards.
* **CASAAL acceptance.** The `.gv` automaton (`casaal_out_neg/<id>.gv` for the negation, `casaal_out/<id>.gv`
  for the formula, `casaal_out_rand/` for the random formulas) is run with its own label parser
  (DNF, `reset(x)`, quoted clock constraints, `{Acc[...]}` marks). An edge is enabled if its cube holds for the
  letter and the clock constraints hold. The run is accepting if an SCC of the loop part contains an edge
  for every acceptance mark of the automaton (generalized Buechi). When CASAAL prints no acceptance mark
  the evaluator uses the same convention as the TChecker adapter (states whose label contains an unbounded
  eventuality are not accepting); this is an adapter assumption.
* **Automata.** `mtl2tba -nopdf` on `f` and `!(f)`, default (optimizer + maximal-delay export) and with
  `-all-copy`: 244 XML files (`automata/`). 87 of the 244 carry location invariants, 97 have two or more
  clocks, 4 have 20 or more locations, and 7 of the default exports contain diagonal guards (F14, R2, R3
  positive; random X31 negated; X62, X64, X83 positive). All were evaluated and agree with the judge.
* **Disagreement handling.** Any automaton whose verdict differs from the judge is minimized (drop positions,
  shrink delays) and logged in `disagreements_<set>.txt`. Both files are empty.

## Validation of the evaluators (so that "no disagreement" is not vacuous)

* `selftest.py`: hand-checked judge cases (interval endpoints, strict and non-strict bounds, hatted
  operators, release, nested). The first run had three failures that were errors in my hand-written
  expectations (the non-hatted `[]` includes the current position; `q ^U other` is true), not judge bugs;
  the expectations were corrected.
* `control.py` / `control_results.tsv`: the automaton of a deliberately mutated formula (off-by-one bound,
  `<=` instead of `<`) is compared with the judge on the original formula, 3,000 words each. mtl2tba evaluator,
  differing words: 38, 97 and 92 (F1 with `<5`, `<=4`, `<=6`), 47 (F4), 60 (F7), 68 (F11), 59 (F12), 5 and 13
  (F5 with `>2`, `>=3`), 2 (N3). Two mutants give 0: F8 (`[]<>[>=2] p` against `>2`) is semantically
  equivalent on infinite words, and F2 as written (`[](p -> [][<=3] q)`, non-hatted) is vacuous because `p` and `q`
  are exclusive letters. So the test detects boundary errors, but its power is low (a few words in 3,000) for
  N3 and F5, where the distinguishing situation is rare. The real run uses 20,000 words per benchmark formula.
* `control_casaal.py` / `control_casaal_results.tsv`: CASAAL is run on mutated formulas and its automaton is compared
  with the judge on the original formula, 5,000 words each: 72, 128, 74, 105, 100, 73 and 13 differing words.
  The CASAAL evaluator therefore detects such errors too.
* 17 of the 100 random formulas are nearly constant on this word class (the negated automaton accepts under 2% or
  over 98% of the words), and 3 of the 22 benchmark patterns (F13, N3, N4). Their positive polarity is equally
  lopsided, so they carry less evidence.

## Limits

* Bounded sample of words: lasso only, at most 9 positions, delays in multiples of 1/2 up to 7 time units
  plus boundary values, strictly increasing timestamps, first event at time > 0. Words with equal timestamps,
  with an event at time 0, or with finer time resolution were not tested. A bug that needs a rare word shape
  may not be found (the controls show the sensitivity for common boundary errors, and it is low for N3 and F5).
* The three evaluators are new unverified code written for this experiment. The judge is a reading of the
  Rocq `msat` definition, cross-checked against hand cases and against the two tools. The tools agree with it,
  which makes a common-mode error in the judge and both tools unlikely but not excluded.
* The CASAAL evaluator parses the `.gv` output; the label grammar was written from the files present
  (`1`, cubes, negated quoted constraints, `reset(x)`), and the no-mark fallback follows the adapter.
* Exclusivity of propositions (CASAAL's domain) is built into the word class, so differences on words with
  several simultaneous propositions are out of scope.
* The earlier `random_diff.tsv` used TChecker product emptiness (all words, symbolic, but through the adapter);
  this experiment uses sampled words and an independent semantics judge. They are complementary.
