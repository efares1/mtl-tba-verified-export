# Experiment 1: seeded-bug mutation study

Question: if a developer writes the optimizer or export passes by hand and slips, how many of the slips do ordinary
tests find, and how many does only a proof exclude?  The 22 mutants are deliberate bugs in OUR tool (the extracted
OCaml in `tool/src/optim.ml`, copy in `~/mut/work/<id>`), not bugs in CASAAL or in Spot.  The Rocq sources were not
mutated and the mutated code was not re-run through Rocq: the statement "excluded by the preservation theorems"
is an argument (each mutated function is one whose language preservation is proved), not a mechanical check.

## Counts (22 mutants)

| outcome | mutants | n |
|---|---|---|
| language-changing, caught by the language test T2 (and by the golden regression T1) | M02 M03 M04 M06 M07 M09 M11 M13 M14 M15 M18 M19 M20 | 13 |
| language-changing, survive T1 and T2 (budget up to 100,000 words per input), exposed only by a hand-built counterexample | M01 M10 M12 M16 | 4 |
| no language change (output form only, or removed check already implied) | M05 M08 M17 M21 M22 | 5 |

So 17 of 22 mutants change the accepted language; T2 finds 13 of 17 (76%) and four (24%) survive everything we ran.
No mutant crashed the tool.  T1 (output differs from the unmutated tool) flags 15 mutants: the same 13 plus M05 and
M17, whose language is unchanged.  T1 is not available to a developer without a trusted reference build; it is a
golden-output regression against the correct tool.

Two further notes.  (1) M16 survives only under the default max-delay split; the all-copy export is unaffected.
(2) M17 is classed equivalent without proof: output differs on 1 of 296 inputs (a redundant disjunct is kept), no
language mismatch was found.

## Tests

- T1: bytes of the output (`.xml`, `.dot`, optimized `.tba`) against the unmutated tool on 148 inputs x 2 export policies
  (296 runs: 28 synthetic + 120 random automata) and on the 21 benchmark patterns (F1-F14, F3b, R1-R3, N1-N3) in both
  polarities with default and `-all-copy` export (84 runs).
- T2: bounded differential language test (`lang_cmp.cpp`) between the input automaton (or, for benchmarks, the unmutated
  `-n 0` export) and the mutant's output, in successive budgets: 1,000 words; 20,000; then 100,000 (corpus) / 20,000
  (benchmarks) words per input, stopping at the first budget with a mismatch.  M01, M05, M08, M10, M12, M16, M17, M21,
  M22 reached the last budget with no mismatch (the first mismatch word index appears in the table).

## Oracle validation and limits

- On the unmutated tool the oracle reports 0 mismatches over the 148 corpus inputs (both export policies) at 1,000 and
  20,000 words, and over the 84 benchmark outputs at 1,000 and 20,000 words.  A first version produced 8 and 30 false
  mismatches on two synthetic automata because it used too few loop iterations before requiring accepting visits; the
  loop count now depends on the largest constant, and the false alarms disappeared.
- Sensitivity: deleting one transition from an automaton is noticed in 354 of 553 attempts at 5,000 words (64%).  The
  oracle is therefore far from complete, which is why four wrong mutants survive; the survivors are not evidence that the
  bugs are benign.
- Words are ultimately periodic (prefix at most 3, loop at most 3 events) with delays in half units from a small set;
  initial valuations: zero plus five random ones.  Büchi acceptance is approximated by requiring an accepting visit in each
  of 12 loop iterations after a warm-up.  Dense-time behaviours needing finer delays or longer loops can be missed.
- The oracle, converters and harness are new unverified code.  The tool is compared against its own semantics as
  read from the exported XML by our converter (guards, invariants, resets, synchronization), not against UPPAAL.
- No third test (T3, e.g. model checking the exported models in UPPAAL/TChecker) was run.  The mutants were chosen by
  us; 22 is a small sample and the survivor rate should not be read as a population estimate.
- Not claimed: that the unmutated tool is free of bugs (it is proved against the Rocq specification; extraction, Spot,
  the HOA reader and the XML printer are outside the proof).

## Reproduce (WSL)

`setup_base.sh` (builds the base tool with a `-dump` option), `python3 make_corpus.py`, `python3 mutants.py build`,
`python3 bench.py` (golden outputs), `python3 run_mutants.py M01 ... M22`, `python3 make_report.py`.
